SEPARATION BY DESIGN

No component completes
the governance chain alone.

JAZB separates who has the right to govern from what machines are technically capable of doing. Human governance establishes legitimate purpose and Law. AI interprets and operates within bounded Authority. Consequential execution, evidence, and assurance remain independently constrained and attributable.

Enterprise Decision Rights

JAZB distinguishes business ownership, Law establishment, architecture, residual-risk acceptance, technical operation, expansion, and independent assurance.

Authority Lifecycle

Purpose and permissible autonomy precede Authority. Law, authorization, delegation, operation, reevaluation, revocation, renewal, and retirement remain governed.

Independent Jurisdictions

Internal Jurisdiction governs enterprise action. Border Jurisdiction governs lawful external passage. Authority in one does not silently authorize the other.

Independent Actuation

Interpretive decisions do not become unrestricted technical power. Actuation validates the authorizing chain, effective target, and permitted effect before execution.

Continuous Assurance

Governance is continuously demonstrated through operation, observation, validation, discovery, remediation, and revalidation as systems and conditions change.

Chain of Authority & Evidence

Law, delegation, decisions, passage, actuation, exceptions, and recovery remain traceable to legitimate provenance and reconstructable evidence.

Keep purpose connected
to operational reality.

Six complementary architecture views expose different stakeholder concerns while describing one governed architecture.

Purpose, desired outcomes, stakeholders, obligations, dependencies, and constraints.

Governance and Decision Rights

Who may establish Law, approve autonomy, accept residual risk, expand Scope, and independently assess governance.

Authority Architecture

Human-Established Law, Rights, Duties, Liberty, delegation, bounded Authority, and provenance.

Operational and Jurisdiction

Internal and Border Jurisdictions, interpretation, Actuation, passage, and actual enforcement paths.

Evidence and Assurance

Decision and action evidence, coverage, uncertainty, limitations, and assurance currency.

Lifecycle and Change

Establishment, operation, material change, reassessment, recovery, reauthorization, renewal, and retirement.

Current State, Target State, and transition: Planned capabilities remain distinguishable from implemented and validated governance. Architecture decisions preserve accountable ownership, assumptions, dependencies, limitations, alternatives, evidence gates, rollback or exit conditions, and reassessment triggers.
Continuous Governance Assurance: Establish → Operate → Observe → Validate → Discover → Remediate → Revalidate. A successful prior test is not permanent assurance, and technical recovery does not automatically restore Authority.
