NIST AI RMF CROSSWALK

Map the alignment.
Preserve the gaps.

The JAZB Framework v1.1 to NIST Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework 1.0 Crosswalk provides an element-level evaluation of all 72 AI RMF Core subcategories against the stable normative requirements of the JAZB Reference Specification.

The crosswalk is designed to show not only where the two frameworks align, but also where they address different governance problems. Mappings are therefore classified conservatively, including explicit No Equivalence findings where JAZB does not normatively address a broader AI risk-management outcome.

PURPOSE

A requirement-level comparison

The crosswalk compares each NIST AI RMF 1.0 Core subcategory with the stable JAZB-RS requirements contained in the JAZB Framework v1.1 Reference Specification.

Its purpose is not to demonstrate that JAZB replaces the AI RMF. Instead, it identifies where JAZB directly aligns with an AI RMF outcome, where the frameworks complement one another, where one may provide implementation support for the other, and where no meaningful equivalence exists.

VALIDATED RESULTS

Conservative classification after internal review

FINAL MAPPING DISTRIBUTION 4 Direct Alignment · 29 Complementary Alignment · 4 Implementation Support · 35 No Equivalence

The formal validation process reduced several stronger preliminary mappings where the JAZB requirement did not fully satisfy the broader AI RMF outcome.

No Equivalence is intentionally preserved as a valid result rather than treating every conceptual similarity as framework alignment.

METHODOLOGY

Source first. JAZB second.

Each mapping begins with the external framework on its own terms. The NIST AI RMF outcome is interpreted first, without attempting to fit it into JAZB terminology.

The relevant JAZB-RS requirements are then evaluated to determine whether the relationship constitutes Direct Alignment, Complementary Alignment, Implementation Support, or No Equivalence.

Mapping confidence, rationale, limitations, and requirement references are retained so that each classification can be independently examined.
